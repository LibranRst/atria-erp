## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: The sheet is genuinely authored for a finance-approval decision at Dekoria for its browsing/review layer — the dark hero card, terracotta-intensity status language, the before→after budget arrow with real nominal values, and the inventory-specific "Inventory / Kas & proyek / Audit" impact triptych are not generic "review modal" fare; no category-interchangeable product ships a stock-opname line-item list with per-unit harga pokok. But the specificity runs out exactly at the commit layer: the approve-confirmation and reject-reason sheets revert to boilerplate ("Setujui pengajuan? Dampak budget sudah ditampilkan di detail...") that names no amount and no counterparty, and the reject-reason placeholder is hardcoded vendor-payment copy shown verbatim even for a stock adjustment. The product's own codebase proves it knows how to branch this content by type — `approval-decision-reason-fields.tsx`'s approval-reason placeholder already does — the reject path just didn't get the same treatment.

**Deterministic scan**: The static CLI detector (`detect.mjs --json`) ran clean — 0 findings across all four source files. This confirms the earlier token/color/radius compliance pass (raw hex, arbitrary shadows, non-token radii) holds up under automated re-scan. The _browser-injected_ detector, which measures actual computed styles in the rendered DOM rather than source patterns, told a very different story: 48 anti-patterns on the vendor-bill sheet, 52 on the inventory-opening sheet (4 extra, specific to its stock-line-item content). The overwhelming majority (40+) are `undersized-ui-text` findings — eyebrow/meta labels at 9-10px, and, most seriously, every secondary/exact Rupiah figure (`money-value-exact` in `app/globals.css`) rendering at **~7.02px** on a 390px viewport. I read the source and confirmed this is not a detector artifact: `.money-value-exact { font-size: clamp(0.4rem, 1.8vw, 0.5625rem); }` — a deliberate, documented shrink-to-fit mechanism (the code comment explains it exists to avoid truncating long Rupiah numbers instead of a fixed size). The intent is reasonable; the calibration isn't — its floor (6.4px) and its `1.8vw` fluid term already put it at ~7px for perfectly ordinary amounts like "Rp 10.000.000" on a standard phone, not just pathological edge cases. This is the single most consequential finding of this critique: the exact figure a user is meant to verify before an irreversible financial commit is close to illegible on the device this product is explicitly built for. Also flagged: one `low-contrast` finding (4.3:1 vs. 4.5:1 needed) on the hero "Perlu ditinjau"/warning badge — close enough to the line that it reads as a token-tuning gap rather than a hardcoded-color mistake. A `nested-cards` finding (white cards inside the dark hero card) and a `layout-transition` finding on `body` are more likely intentional hierarchy / unrelated global chrome respectively — noted but not treated as defects.

**Visual overlays**: Live overlay injection succeeded on both sheets (confirmed via console capture, not claimed from source alone); no persistent `[Human]` tab was left open since this critique ran through sub-agents rather than an interactive session — the verbatim console findings above are the record of what the overlay reported.

## Overall Impression

This is a well-composed, domain-specific review surface let down at exactly the two moments that matter most: the instant of commit (which says less about the decision than the screen before it) and the literal readability of the number being committed to (which shrinks below a legible floor on the exact device class this product targets). Fix those two things and this sheet moves from "Acceptable" into "Good" territory without touching anything else.

## What's Working

1. **`ApprovalImpactReview`'s before/after budget block** — "Saat ini Rp 76 jt (30%) → Jika disetujui Rp 86 jt (34%)" with a directional arrow between two `MoneyValue` stacks is exactly the purpose-built comparison DESIGN.md calls for, and it answers the real question ("what does approving this do to my budget") instead of a generic progress bar.
2. **The type-aware branch for `inventory_opening`** — swapping the budget-percentage geometry for three plain-language impact statements (Inventory / Kas & proyek / Audit) recognizes that a stock adjustment has no budget-consumption story rather than forcing one onto it.
3. **`approvalRequiresOpeningReason`** — requiring a written justification specifically for stock-balance approvals (which bypass the normal cash/vendor audit trail) is a thoughtful, domain-aware guardrail rather than a blanket rule applied everywhere.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Exact Rupiah amounts render near-illegible (~7px) on mobile.** Every secondary/full amount in this sheet — the hero card's exact value under the compact one, every "Budget kategori"/"Saat ini"/"Jika disetujui" figure in the impact section — uses `.money-value-exact`'s `clamp(0.4rem, 1.8vw, 0.5625rem)`, measured live at 7.02px on a 390px viewport. Why it matters: this is a finance-approval screen where the _exact_ number is the thing a user must verify before an irreversible, audit-logged commit; sub-8px text fails basic legibility for a field user glancing at a phone outdoors. The mechanism (shrink instead of truncate) is reasonable, but the floor is miscalibrated and already triggers on ordinary amounts, not just edge cases. Fix: raise the clamp floor to something legible (≥10-11px) and let genuinely extreme values fall back to truncation with the full value available on tap/tooltip, rather than shrinking indefinitely. This is a shared component (`page-primitives.tsx`), so the fix benefits every `MoneyValue` usage app-wide, not just this sheet. Suggested command: `/impeccable typeset`.

**[P0] Approve confirmation can be dismissed mid-submit with no guard.** `ConfirmationSheet`'s backdrop-click and Escape handlers close the dialog unconditionally, with no `if (pending) return` — even though its own "Batal" button is correctly disabled while pending. Why it matters: a stray tap outside the dialog or an accidental Escape while the approval is still being written closes the UI with no signal whether the audit-logged mutation completed — the worst possible ambiguity at the exact moment of an irreversible commit. Fix: guard the backdrop/Escape handlers the same way `approval-list.tsx`'s own `closeDecision` already does. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`.

**[P1] Commit steps don't restate what's being decided.** Neither the approve-confirmation dialog nor the reject-reason sheet shows the amount, vendor, or project again — confirmed live on both the Rp 10 jt vendor bill and the Rp 10,1 jt stock item. Why it matters: this is exactly where error prevention matters most; PRODUCT.md itself treats Owner approval as a hard gate for payments ≥ Rp 5.000.000, and the last screen before that gate closes says less than the screen the user just left. Fix: pass the item's amount and description/vendor into both sheets' header or description line. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.

**[P1] Reject-reason placeholder is hardcoded to vendor language for every item type.** `approval-list.tsx`'s rejection `Textarea` placeholder ("nominal belum sesuai dengan progres pekerjaan") is static regardless of `item.type` — confirmed live that it shows unchanged on the inventory-opening item, where "progres pekerjaan" is meaningless. Why it matters: the approval-reason field one component over already branches its placeholder by type for exactly this reason — this is an inconsistency the codebase itself proves is avoidable, and it's a visible design-specificity gap at the flow's most emotionally loaded step. Fix: branch the placeholder by `decision.item.type`, mirroring `approval-decision-reason-fields.tsx`'s existing pattern. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.

**[P2] Emergency override doesn't carry visual weight proportional to its risk.** The "Gunakan emergency override" toggle — which bypasses a normal self-approval restriction — renders as an ordinary `SwitchRow` with no distinct terracotta-critical emphasis or extra friction. Why it matters: this is a control-integrity concern, not just a cosmetic one; a time-pressured Owner self-approving should feel more resistance here than for a routine approval, not the same amount. Fix: give the override row critical-tone treatment (icon/border) proportional to the button hierarchy used elsewhere for irreversible actions. Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`.

## Persona Red Flags

**Owner scanning multiple pending items in ~30 seconds** (PRODUCT.md's own target): the commit dialogs' failure to restate amount/vendor (P1) directly undermines this persona's ability to sanity-check against several similar Rp-10-juta-range items in one sitting — and the 7px exact figures (P0) make the one number that would disambiguate them hardest to read at exactly the moment it matters most.

**Field approver, one-handed, outdoors, under time pressure**: the dismissible-mid-pending confirm dialog (P0) is precisely the failure mode this persona hits — a thumb slip on a bright screen easily lands on the backdrop, with no reassurance the tap registered or was lost.

**Owner performing an emergency self-approval override**: gets no proportional friction (P2) — flips a control that bypasses a normal restriction with the same visual and interaction cost as an ordinary setting toggle.

## Minor Observations

- "Alasan Penolakan" is the one Title Case outlier among otherwise consistent sentence-case sheet titles ("Detail persetujuan", "Konteks pengajuan", "Dampak ke budget").
- The hero card's urgency badge (due-date-driven) and the impact section's budget badge (percentage-driven) are independent signals that can read as contradictory on a fast scan (e.g., "Perlu perhatian" shown above a budget section reporting "Tetap dalam batas budget") — the connecting logic is present in copy but requires reading both to reconcile.
- Disabled "Tolak pengajuan" (`bg-error` at 40% opacity, white text) renders as a washed pink that reads more like "already failed" than "not yet valid."
- The rejection minimum-character requirement differs by type (10 for inventory, 5 otherwise) but is only surfaced via toast after a short submission attempt, not proactively before typing.
- `low-contrast` (4.3:1 vs. 4.5:1) on the "Perlu ditinjau"/warning badge — one token step away from AA compliance, worth a contrast-ratio pass on the `warning`/`warning-container` pair.
- `project_cost` ("Biaya lapangan") could not be verified live — the queue had zero pending field-expense items during this review (confirmed via the "Lapangan" filter). It shares `ApprovalImpactReview`'s budget-percentage branch with `vendor_payment`, differing only by icon and label; whether a self-submitted field expense should get different scrutiny than a third-party vendor bill (e.g., surfacing "Dokumen pendukung" more prominently when a receipt is more likely to be missing) is worth deciding deliberately rather than by schema convenience — see the questions below.

## Questions to Consider

1. If the confirmation dialog is the one and only checkpoint before an irreversible, audit-logged entry, why does it currently say _less_ about the decision than the screen the user just came from?
2. The hero card answers "how urgent" (due-date) while the impact section answers "is this within budget" (percentage) — is showing two independent status signals per item actually helping the 30-second-scan Owner, or would one reconciled status line serve better?
3. `project_cost` and `vendor_payment` currently render through an identical impact layout — is that intentional (a field expense and a vendor bill carry the same approval risk), or is it schema convenience masking a real gap in how self-submitted claims should be scrutinized differently from third-party invoices?
